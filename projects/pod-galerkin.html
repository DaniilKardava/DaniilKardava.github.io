Research context

Learn a smaller space without discarding the physics.

Earlier Clarkson workQuantum Element Method ↗ Workflow overviewPhysics-driven POD-Galerkin ↗

The Schrödinger equation governs the quantum behavior of atoms, molecules, semiconductor nanostructures, quantum wells, and quantum dots. Designing and simulating these systems often requires solving it repeatedly, and this project is designed to accelerate that process.

Solving the Schrödinger equation typically produces a large matrix eigenvalue problem. These numerical methods can be highly accurate, but repeating the solve across many configurations quickly becomes expensive.

We often care primarily about the leading eigenvalues and the eigenspace spanned by their eigenvectors. The POD-Galerkin workflow learns a compact basis for this important subspace from previously computed solutions. Restricting the original matrix to that basis produces a much smaller eigenproblem, with dimension roughly comparable to the number of eigenvalues sought.

My contribution

Replace one growing global solve with a sequence of local ones.

The traditional workflow builds a single reduced space large enough to contain all K eigenvectors we want to compute. It then diagonalizes a dense reduced matrix whose dimension grows with K; because dense diagonalization is cubic in matrix dimension, this creates an O(K3) scaling bottleneck.

We are developing a statewise alternative whose measured cost grows approximately linearly with K over the tested range. This makes it possible to extend the POD-Galerkin workflow toward problems that require a very large number of eigenstates.

What comes next

Turn improved scaling into a robust solver.

The next work is concentrated at the difficult end of the spectrum: improving state tracking near ambiguous and nearly degenerate eigenpairs, reducing the remaining selection cost, and extending matched DNS validation cleanly through all 3,000 requested states.

The core result is already visible: organizing the reduced model around individual state families changes how the online solve scales, creating a viable path toward quantum calculations that require many eigenstates.

Active research: source code and full server artifacts are currently maintained in a private research repository.
