The idea

Turn a hard constraint into a temperature.

Directly counting non-overlapping placements becomes difficult as the grid and fleet grow. Instead, the model allows overlaps and assigns an energy to them. A Gibbs sampler removes one ship at a time and redraws its position conditioned on the rest of the board.

P(s | others) ∝ exp(−β E(s))

At low inverse temperature β, collisions are common. Raising β suppresses them until the distribution concentrates on zero-overlap configurations. Average energy along that path is connected to the change in the log partition function.

d log Z(β) / dβ = −𝔼β[E]

Implementation

Prototype first. Then remove work.

The optimization strategy was driven by the structure of the sampler: each move changes only a few squares, and energy takes values in a small discrete range.

Python

NumPy prototype established the grid representation, energy models, and Gibbs update.

C++

The sampler was ported for larger experiments and profiled iteratively.

Local updates

Only candidate placements intersecting a changed square have their energy updated.

Buckets

Placements are grouped by integer energy, replacing repeated full-vector probability calculations.

Sampling

A custom weighted draw selects an energy bucket and then a placement within it.

Exact validation

The sampler tracks ground truth.

For three labeled length-2 ships on a 4×4 board, all 13,824 configurations can be enumerated. A fixed-seed run used 4,000 independently initialized samples at each of 25 β values and closely followed the exact Boltzmann expectation.
